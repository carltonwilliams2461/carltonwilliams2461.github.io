THE METHOD

Why a cheap rate can still cost you money

A load paying $2.50 a mile sounds strong — until 150 deadhead miles, a 2% factoring cut, and a $4.00 fuel price pull the real profit per mile under your cost. MileMoney runs every load against the whole picture, not the headline rate.

  1. Know your cost per mile first. Fixed costs spread across your monthly miles, plus fuel, maintenance, tires, and a repairs reserve on every mile.
  2. Count every mile, not just loaded miles. Deadhead burns the same fuel and tires and pays nothing. Profit is measured on total miles.
  3. Recover fuel spikes with a surcharge. When the pump price rises above your base, the surcharge formula keeps that increase off your back.
  4. Price from your break-even rate up. The break-even rate is the floor. Your target profit belongs in the rate before you accept the load, not as a hope afterward.

QUESTIONS

Built for owner-operators and small fleets

Does MileMoney store my numbers?

No. Calculations happen locally in this page. The current version has no account, database, analytics, or server-side form submission.

Can I file IFTA with this?

No. The IFTA tool is an estimator for planning and record-checking. File through your base jurisdiction's official IFTA process with your actual trip and fuel records.

How could this site earn revenue?

Natural paths include display advertising after traffic is established, affiliate links to factoring, ELD, fuel-card, and dispatch software, a premium spreadsheet pack, or white-label licensing to carriers and trucking associations.

What should be added next?

Likely expansions are a detention-pay tracker, per-diem and tax-reserve estimators, lease-purchase payoff checks, and state-by-state IFTA pages with current published rates.
