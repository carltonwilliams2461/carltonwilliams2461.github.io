THE METHOD

Why a weighted GPA is not just a bigger number

An unweighted GPA treats every course the same. A weighted GPA gives extra credit for harder courses — typically +0.5 for Honors and +1.0 for AP — so a B in AP Chemistry can count like an A in a regular class. Neither number is “the real one”; colleges and schools each decide which they use, which is why GradePoint always shows both, with total credits, so nothing is hidden.

  1. Start with the letter. Every letter converts to grade points on the 4.0 scale before anything else happens.
  2. Weight by credits. A 4-credit course moves the GPA four times as much as a 1-credit course — credits are the volume knob.
  3. Add rigor honestly. Honors and AP boosts apply only to passing grades, and no course can exceed 5.0 weighted points.
  4. Check the final early. The score you need on a final is fixed by three numbers — current grade, exam weight, and target. Know it before finals week, not during it.

QUESTIONS

Built for teachers, students, tutors, and homeschool families

Does GradePoint store my grades?

No. Every calculation happens locally in this page. The current version has no account, database, analytics, or server-side form submission — close the tab and the numbers are gone.

Is this my official GPA?

No. Schools calculate GPA with their own scales, rounding, and course rules. Use GradePoint to plan and estimate, then confirm official figures with your school or counselor.
